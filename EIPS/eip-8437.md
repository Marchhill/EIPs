## Abstract

This EIP defines `eth/73`, a version of the [`eth` wire protocol](https://github.com/ethereum/devp2p/blob/2c19a28b25c29487773ad6b07243e290fa8d65ec/caps/eth.md) that propagates [EIP-8288](./eip-8288.md) proof wrappers ("wrappers" below). Peers announce wrapper modes, lengths and hashes, then retrieve wrappers by hash. A separate mode carries raw signatures for recognized generic leaf programs, for proving after relay.

## Motivation

EIP-8288 separates signatures and proofs from transaction envelopes and permits aggregation across transactions. Existing transaction propagation does not identify this shared proof material, so peers need a way to discover and retrieve it.

Users of other signature schemes may be able to sign without having the resources to produce a STARK. Native verification of raw signatures lets them reach provers through the public mempool. Blocks still require ordinary generic proof coverage.

## Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119) and [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174).

`eth/73` extends `eth/72` ([EIP-8070](./eip-8070.md)), using its notation and message semantics except as specified below. Status (`0x00`) is unchanged; the protocol version and new message IDs are provisional. Request IDs follow [EIP-2481](./eip-2481.md). Clients SHOULD advertise `eth/73` once EIP-8288 is scheduled.

### New messages

Messages use canonical RLP with the structures below and no extra fields or trailing bytes.

Until EIP-8288 is enabled for local mempool validation, clients MUST NOT send wrapper messages and MUST ignore received wrapper messages without penalties; underlying transport size limits still apply.

#### NewProofWrapperHashes (0x16)

`[[mode₀: P, length₀: P, hash₀: B_32, deps-hash₀: B_32, txs-hash₀: B_32], ...]`

Announce 1–64 held wrappers in strictly ascending hash order: mode is `0`, `1` or `2`, length is 1–`MAX_WRAPPER_BYTES` as defined by [EIP-8288](./eip-8288.md#constants), and hash is Keccak-256 of the complete canonical RLP wrapper, including pooled transaction encodings. Peers SHOULD announce newly validated wrappers to every connected `eth/73` peer and, on connection, up to 64 offered wrappers covering at least one pending or raw-held transaction, most recent first.

Clients MUST ignore announcements of unsupported modes without penalties after checking the message's encoding, count, length and ordering. Clients recognizing no programs MUST likewise ignore mode 2 announcements without fetching them.

`deps-hash` is Keccak-256 of the concatenated 96-byte entries in the canonical `deps` list; `txs-hash` is Keccak-256 of the concatenated, ascending consensus transaction hashes. Requesters MAY skip a fetch when both match a held wrapper validated under the current rules, subject to mode 2's coverage rules below. Hints alone establish no coverage or validity; matching only `deps-hash` does not identify the transactions.

Under the same verification rules, peers SHOULD suppress hashes already exchanged on the connection and wrappers whose transactions are covered by held, validated wrappers exchanged with or announced by the recipient, unless they reduce proof bytes or estimated verification work, or provide an IL-bounded mode-1 proof where existing coverage cannot. Peers MAY re-announce a hash once a local interval has passed or it has left their bounded record of the connection.

The announcement tuple is fixed for `eth/73`. Extending it requires a new protocol version; canonical decoding rejects extra fields.

#### GetProofWrappers (0x17)

`[request-id: P, compact: P, [hash₀: B_32, hash₁: B_32, ...]]`

Request 1 to 32 distinct wrapper hashes per message, each previously announced by the recipient on that connection under the same verification rules. Responders MUST treat hashes they have not announced as unavailable and MUST NOT penalize the request, since either side's record of announcements may be bounded. `compact` is `0` or `1`: with `0`, returned wrappers MUST contain complete transaction encodings; with `1`, responders MAY use hash references as below.

#### ProofWrappers (0x18)

`[request-id: P, [[hash₀: B_32, wrapper₀], [hash₁: B_32, wrapper₁], ...]]`

Return available validated wrappers as embedded [EIP-8288 RLP items](./eip-8288.md#mempool-wrapper-object), extended by mode 2 below, paired with their canonical hashes, in request order with omissions allowed and no repeats. The recommended soft response limit is 2 MiB. Responders SHOULD stop after reaching it, returning complete wrappers only. Empty responses are permitted.

Requesters match distinct requested hashes in order, reconstruct wrappers as below, check canonical hashes, modes and full lengths against the recipient's announcements, and re-request hashes after the last returned wrapper. Earlier omissions are unavailable from that peer. Requesters SHOULD fetch each wrapper from one announcer at a time and try another after an empty response or timeout.

Responders whose serving budget is spent SHOULD return an empty response rather than omit wrappers or stay silent. Requesters SHOULD NOT penalize an empty response or count it toward a limit on sources tried for a wrapper. They MAY score earlier omissions in a nonempty response in proportion to the requested wrappers withheld, and SHOULD count an unanswered request against the peer. A future version may add an explicit retry-after hint.

#### Hash-based omission

- With `compact = 1`, responders MAY replace transaction bytes previously sent in full within a validated wrapper on that connection with `[encoding-hash: B_32]`, if smaller. The hash is Keccak-256 of the pooled transaction bytes, excluding the enclosing RLP string prefix. References are RLP lists; transactions are byte strings.
- Resolve references from cached, validated encodings and check their hashes. The complete wrapper determines identity and byte limits; reconstruction MUST stay within the announced length.
- On a missing encoding, requesters MUST discard the compact item and SHOULD retry with `compact = 0` and a fresh request ID. Cache misses MUST NOT incur penalties.

### Raw witnesses for recognized leaf programs

Mode `2` carries native-verifiable witnesses for ordinary `LEANSTARK_SCHEME` dependencies. It permits holding and relaying transactions before proof-backed pool admission, without changing block validity, gas or the aggregation circuit. The program's generic proof statement MUST be enabled under the active consensus rules; transport support alone is insufficient. Clients MAY support a subset of recognized programs or ignore mode 2.

#### Encoding

`[transactions, 2, [deps, witnesses]]`

- `transactions` and `deps` follow the canonical wrapper encoding. A mode 2 wrapper MUST contain at most 64 transactions and 16 raw entries, including transactions sharing a dependency. `witnesses` has one entry per dependency, in the same order.
- A raw entry is `[public-key: B, message: B_32, signature: B]`, for a generic dependency whose `verification_key_hash` identifies a locally recognized program. Its complete RLP encoding MUST be at most 16 KiB.
- Other entries are ordinary mode 0 witness byte strings, subject to mode 0's per-scheme limits; raw entries do not consume the direct generic-proof allowance. At least one entry MUST be raw. EIP-8288's per-transaction limits still apply, so mixing raw and proven generic dependencies may require separate transactions.

The canonical hash and announced length include all raw witnesses. Hash-based omission applies only to transaction encodings.

#### Recognized programs

A recognized-program descriptor pins the exact EIP-8288 verification-key hash, signature parameters and encodings, message semantics, claim computation and leaf public statement. Initial profiles are ML-DSA-44, Falcon-512 and SLH-DSA-SHAKE-128s, specified in [recognized programs](../assets/eip-8437/recognized-programs.md).

Before enabling a descriptor, clients MUST have its exact key and conformance vectors establishing that every native-accepted witness can produce the same claim in the leaf. Native policy MAY reject additional signature encodings. The program MUST fit the active generic verifier's resource bounds. Clients MUST NOT download or execute verifier code supplied by peers.

Recognized keys are client policy, not a fork-managed consensus list. Adding or replacing a descriptor requires compatible client support, not a new consensus scheme or aggregation key. An unsupported key MAY be declined without penalties; changing recognition MUST invalidate affected cached raw-witness verdicts.

#### Holding and proving

- Check every witness and the transactions' static validity before holding or relay; each raw claim MUST equal the dependency's resolved `data_hash`. Native signature validity alone does not authorize a transaction. Full account validation, balance and replacement checks are REQUIRED before pool admission once proof coverage arrives; optional earlier filters confer no admission. Held entries MUST NOT reserve balances or replace proof-backed pool entries, and MUST be evicted on expiry.
- Raw candidates MUST have separate byte, validation-work and proving-work budgets globally and per node identity, retained across reconnects, with capacity reserved for local submissions and proof-backed traffic. Budget exhaustion permits refusal without penalties.
- Raw validation establishes no proof coverage for block or inclusion-list validation. A raw entry MUST NOT be supplied to the recursive verifier as a proof. Builders or other provers must produce the corresponding leaf proof and ordinary aggregate coverage before inclusion; they MAY publish mode 1 wrappers for reuse. Multi-claim proving is usable only where the active generic statement supports it.
- A held mode 2 wrapper MUST NOT suppress fetching or announcing a mode 0 or 1 wrapper by coverage, even when both hints match. Mode 0/1 coverage may suppress mode 2; mode 2 coverage may suppress other mode 2 wrappers under the normal rules. Exact-hash duplicate suppression applies to all modes.

On block-producing nodes, speculative raw-witness proving SHOULD yield resources to block-proof construction and required inclusion-list integration.

Supporting native checks does not require running a prover. Holding and relay promise neither proof production nor inclusion-list protection: only a covering proof can establish the latter under EIP-8288.

### Transaction propagation

Dependency-bearing transactions MUST NOT appear in Transactions (`0x02`) or PooledTransactions (`0x0a`) on any version; GetPooledTransactions (`0x09`) treats them as unavailable. NewPooledTransactionHashes (`0x08`) MUST NOT announce them except for blob availability below.

#### Blob-carrying dependency transactions

On `eth/73`, EIP-8070 applies to blob-carrying frame transactions. ProofWrappers carries [EIP-8141's pooled network form](./eip-8141.md#networking), with blob data elided as specified by [EIP-8070](./eip-8070.md#execution-clients--devp2p-changes).

- After wrapper validation and EIP-8070's checks, clients SHOULD advertise cells through NewPooledTransactionHashes, with covering-wrapper announcements subject to suppression. Unknown frame hashes may elicit empty GetPooledTransactions responses.
- GetCells/Cells use consensus transaction hashes. Dependency-bearing transactions require a validated covering wrapper before cell retrieval and EIP-8070's checks before admission. Mode 2 validation permits cell retrieval, but pool admission still requires proof coverage. Validated wrappers may circulate before availability is established; their announcements do not count as providers.

### Validation

- Clients MUST check the complete wrapper's encoding, length and hash and validate under EIP-8288, or the mode 2 rules above, before announcement, serving, reaggregation or transaction admission. Until validation completes, received wrapper data MUST remain in bounded request-scoped storage and be discarded on cancellation or timeout.

- Validate using the EIP-8288 fork rules selected for local mempool validation. If those rules change, for example through a new proof verifier or dependency limits, wrappers validated only under the previous rules MUST NOT be served, announced or used to suppress announcements.

- Before decoding or hashing wrapper bodies, requesters SHOULD check that the response's request ID is outstanding and its connection open; responders SHOULD skip requests from closed connections.

- Transport item-count, hard byte-length and response-order violations SHOULD cause disconnection. Malformed transport encodings, unmatched or excess responses, hash, mode or length mismatches, and references in a `compact = 0` response MUST be discarded and MAY cause penalties or disconnection. Crossing the soft response limit alone MUST NOT cause penalties. A complete wrapper that is not canonical for its mode is likewise malformed.

- In modes 0 and 2, a mismatch between dependency and witness counts is malformed encoding. Exceeding mode 2's transaction or raw-entry count is a hard item-count violation.

- Once the canonical wrapper is reconstructed, clients MUST check its announcement digests alongside its hash, before proof verification. A mismatch MUST cause discard and SHOULD cause disconnection, independently of proof validity.

- Pool rejection of an individual transaction does not invalidate its covering wrapper and MUST NOT incur peer penalties. Clients SHOULD relay such a wrapper unchanged only if it newly admitted at least one transaction and no fewer than it rejected; already-pending transactions count as neither. For mode 2, apply this relay rule to acceptance into raw holding instead. Holding MUST NOT prevent relay when a transaction later obtains proof coverage and enters the pool.

- Proof, raw-signature, claim or dependency-rule failures, including exceeding `MAX_SIGS_PER_TX` or another EIP-8288 dependency limit, MUST cause the wrapper to be discarded without peer penalties or disconnection for that failure. Such wrappers are invalid, not malformed: only failures of the transport, encoding, hash, mode, length and digest checks above are malformed.

- Clients MAY deprioritize or rate-limit fetching from peers whose wrappers repeatedly fail validation or whose requests go unanswered, MAY disconnect such peers past a local threshold, and SHOULD retain this standing by node identity across reconnections.

- Clients SHOULD bound served ProofWrappers bytes globally and divide the budget fairly between peers, for example 32 MiB/s in total and at most 8 MiB/s per peer.

## Rationale

Proof-backed wrappers carry transactions alongside their dependency proofs so receivers can validate them together. Separate propagation would require additional mempool state for envelopes awaiting coverage, or fetching those envelopes again after the proof arrives. Mode 2 explicitly holds native-checked transactions awaiting proving.

Reusing EIP-8070's cell announcements preserves its provider and sampler behavior. Putting cell masks in wrappers would make wrapper identity depend on each peer's changing holdings.

Mode distinguishes direct-witness, recursive-proof and recognized raw-signature verification; length gives the complete wrapper's encoded size. Dependency and transaction digests identify equivalent coverage despite different proof bytes, avoiding redundant downloads. Compared with a dependency hint alone, `txs-hash` adds 33 encoded bytes per announcement to distinguish different transactions sharing dependencies. The wire dependency digest uses a fixed hash of encoded triples rather than the circuit digest, allowing checks independent of fork verification rules.

Hash omission saves repeated transaction bytes while preserving canonical identity, at the cost of cache tracking and full retries after eviction. Encoding hashes also bind blob metadata.

A distinct raw-witness mode separates native acceptance from proof coverage and lets unsupported peers ignore it without changing mode 0. Recognizing a key selects an existing generic statement; it adds no signature scheme to consensus. Native implementations avoid distributing executable admission programs, at the cost of client updates and conformance testing for each recognized program.

Raw relay saves users from proving and avoids transmitting individual STARKs. Provers still bear the work and may require tips or a separate service arrangement; relay-only nodes need no such arrangement. Proving may batch claims where supported, but the transport also works with singleton proofs.

Raw submission limits bound both signature checks and transaction processing: a shared dependency does not make additional transactions free to validate. Recognized programs define their own claims, avoiding an extra network-mandated hash inside the leaf.

## Backwards Compatibility

`eth/73` may be negotiated before EIP-8288 activates. Older versions continue exchanging blocks and other transactions but receive neither wrappers nor dependency-bearing envelopes through transaction messages.

Peers without mode 2 support ignore its announcements. Modes 0 and 1 retain their encodings and proof requirements; program-recognition updates do not activate the generic consensus scheme.

## Test Cases

[Announcement vectors](../assets/eip-8437/announcement-vectors.json), [wrapper vectors](../assets/eip-8437/wrapper-vectors.json) and [the checker](../assets/eip-8437/check_vectors.py) cover the following encoding and matching cases; synthetic envelopes and witnesses do not test transaction or proof validity.

| Checker case | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum mode 0/1 announcement/request; excess count | 6,787/1,072 encoded bytes for 64 announcements/32 hashes; reject excess |
| Unsupported modes, alone or mixed with supported modes | Ignore unsupported entries; still enforce message encoding, count, length and ordering |
| Request a received announcement; request an unannounced hash | Requester selects the former; responder omits the latter |
| Changed enclosed envelope, proof bytes or announced mode/length | Reject mismatch |
| Different proof bytes, identical dependency and transaction sets | Different wrapper hashes; equal coverage hints permit skipping the fetch |
| Equal dependency digest, different transaction sets | Transaction digests differ; dependency equality alone does not suppress fetching |
| False dependency or transaction digest, including with an invalid proof | Reject mismatch before proof verification |
| Empty envelopes, unresolved references in a canonical wrapper, duplicate or reordered envelopes | Reject malformed or noncanonical wrapper |
| Full response to either request flag, without omission caches | Accept |
| Compact response with cached references; missing reference | Reconstruct the identical canonical wrapper; discard the incomplete item and retry in full |
| Reference when `compact = 0`, wrong cached encoding or false canonical hash | Reject |
| Compact form fits the byte cap but reconstructed wrapper exceeds it | Reject |
| Blob network wrapper with elided data, commitments and cell proofs | Accept encoding; use the consensus transaction hash for ordering and cell requests |
| Nonempty blob data, mismatched commitments or wrong proof counts | Reject wrapper; metadata changes also change wrapper identity |
| Complete wrapper exactly at 2 MiB; one byte larger | Accept the size boundary, including response overhead; reject the oversized wrapper |
| Response crosses soft limit on final wrapper | Stop packing; receiver accepts overshoot |
| Response omits whole wrappers, repeats or reorders them | Accept omissions; reject repeats/reordering |
| Mode 2 raw entry for each initial profile; mixed raw and ordinary witnesses | Accept the encoding; signature and claim validity require program conformance vectors |
| Mode 2 with two raw entries and one direct generic proof in separate transactions | Accept within mode 2 limits; raw entries do not consume the direct-proof allowance |
| Raw entry in mode 0; mode 2 with no raw entry, wrong tuple shape or an entry over 16 KiB | Reject malformed wrapper |
| Missing or excess witnesses in mode 0 or 2 | Reject malformed encoding in both modes |
| 64 transactions sharing one raw dependency; 65 transactions; 17 raw entries | Accept the count boundary; reject both excess counts |
| Mode 2 announcement to a peer recognizing no programs | Ignore without fetching or penalizing; retain normal transport checks |
| Mode 2 and mode 1 with identical coverage hints | Raw-only coverage cannot suppress mode 1; proven coverage may suppress mode 2 |

Client integration cases require peer and fork state not modeled by the checker:

- Suppress wrappers covering already-covered transactions unless cheaper or newly IL-reusable.
- Hints never establish coverage before validation. A correct hint remains consistent across verifier changes; an incorrect hint is penalizable independently of proof failure.
- Before local activation, ignore wrapper messages without penalties and send none.
- Discard a wrapper failing proof or dependency validation, including one exceeding `MAX_SIGS_PER_TX`, without penalizing its sender, including across a verifier change; malformed transport or mismatched announced hashes remain penalizable.
- Omit an encoding previously sent in full on the connection; retain its cache entry for reconstruction or retry in full after eviction. Never omit an encoding not previously delivered.
- A valid wrapper remains relayable unchanged when one transaction fails pool admission; that rejection does not penalize the sender.
- A complete wrapper can be validated without envelope requests; cancelled or timed-out validation releases its request buffer without admitting transactions.
- Exhaust per-peer and global verification budgets with direct mode 0 witnesses and singleton or overlapping mode 1 wrappers. Defer or decline further work without declaring unvalidated wrappers invalid or penalizing senders solely for budget exhaustion.
- An unknown blob frame hash triggers an empty GetPooledTransactions response; its covering wrapper later arrives. Fetch cells only after validating it; admit only after EIP-8070's checks.
- Multiple wrappers covering A do not add providers or reset sampling. Two peers' full-cell announcements count as two providers; invalid cells do not invalidate the dependency proof or unrelated transactions.
- A native-accepted witness produces the same claim in its leaf. Native policy may reject an encoding the leaf accepts; an incorrect claim is not held, and native acceptance does not bypass account authorization at pool admission.
- Ignore an unsupported mode 2 announcement; decline an unrecognized key without penalties. Recognition changes invalidate cached raw verdicts.
- A native-validated raw transaction creates no inclusion-list obligation. A subsequent covering proof triggers full pool checks; raw-only holders still fetch it and relay valid newly admitted transactions.
- Reconnect after exhausting a raw budget; the same node identity retains its accounting. Remote exhaustion leaves the local and proof-backed reservations available. Expiry evicts held transactions.
- Announce held raw transactions to a new connection. A held transaction cannot replace a pool entry or reserve its payer's balance; block-proof work takes precedence over speculative raw proving.

## Security Considerations

Clients should bound announcements, outstanding requests, buffered and cached bytes, verification/proving work and serving traffic per peer and globally. Direct mode 0 witness verification should have an explicit rate limit; singleton and overlapping mode 1 wrappers also consume the verification budget. Clients should prefer mode 1 when it reduces verification work for the covered transactions. Budget values are local policy, informed by verification-only measurements. Exhaustion permits local refusal without making a wrapper invalid or implying peer misbehavior.

Request deadlines limit withholding; large responses delay other messages. Validation before relay adds per-hop download and verification latency; propagation within the aggregation interval requires measurements. EIP-8070's blobpool safeguards apply. Empty responses and responses cut at the soft limit alone should not incur penalties.

Every native-accepted witness must be provable as the same claim under the pinned key; otherwise it can poison proving queues. Native policy may be stricter than the leaf, and cannot repair an unsound leaf verifier. Provers should recheck accepted witnesses with the leaf's semantics before proving. Per-profile benchmarks must include time and peak memory: ML-DSA proving can require seconds and multi-gigabyte working memory even for one signature, while other profiles have different costs. Small witnesses do not imply cheap proving. Signers depend on prover availability until coverage exists and should retain a self-proving or account-authorized fallback. Proving concentration and work wasted on later-invalidated transactions remain risks.
